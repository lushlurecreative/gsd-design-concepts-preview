Two liveaboards, sixteen guests each

What you came for

The animals of the northern islands

Over thirty shark species have been identified in Galápagos waters. These are the encounters the northern circuit is built around.

Whale shark

June – November

IUCN Red List Endangered. Nearly 99% of the whale sharks visiting Darwin are adult females. Each one is identified by the spot pattern behind its gills.

Scalloped hammerhead

Year-round, schooling by day

The schools that make Wolf and Darwin famous. Telemetry has recorded the species diving to 1,000 m at night around Malpelo and Cocos.

Oceanic manta ray

Cape Marshall & City of Mantas

Encountered at cleaning stations, usually shallow. The species has been recorded on dives beyond 1,200 m.

Mola mola

Punta Vicente Roca, cold water

The sunfish cleaning station on Isabela's western side. A Galápagos tracking study recorded one individual at 1,112 m.

Galápagos shark

Year-round

The archipelago's namesake requiem shark, common on the exposed northern walls alongside silkies and blacktips.

Spotted eagle ray

Year-round

Photo-identified from its ventral spot pattern, one of the species guests are asked to photograph and submit.

Galápagos sea lion

Cousin's Rock & throughout

Curious, fast and entirely uninterested in your dive plan. Reliable at Cousin's Rock on the way home.

Marine iguana

Cabo Douglas, Fernandina

The only lizard in the world that feeds underwater — grazing algae off the rock while you hold station beside it.

The dive sites on every itinerary

Darwin — the Pillars of Evolution

Four dives on day three, two more on day four. Whale sharks and hammerhead schools in season; the arch itself partially collapsed in 2021.

Wolf Island

Two dives on day four and three on day five. Very high shark biomass and strong currents.

Cape Marshall & City of Mantas

Day two, three dives. Manta rays on Isabela's eastern side.

Punta Vicente Roca

Day six. A cold-water mola mola cleaning station; also penguins, seahorses and the endemic bullhead shark.

Cabo Douglas, Fernandina

Day six. Marine iguanas feeding underwater.

Cousin's Rock

Day seven, two dives, followed by a Santa Cruz highlands land excursion.

Citizen science

Dive with a Purpose

Since 2019 a Galápagos Whale Shark Project scientist has sailed on every Citizen Science departure. Guests aren't spectators to that work — the photographs and counts you take go into it.

  • Photo-identification: whale sharks by the spot pattern behind the gills, turtles by facial pattern, mantas and rays by ventral spots. Submissions feed Sharkbook.ai, the international photo-ID database.
  • SharkCount: standardised observation counts recorded across 20 dive sites in the archipelago.
  • Satellite tagging and in-water behavioural research, led by the project team aboard.
  • Dive Against Debris: underwater debris data collected with Project AWARE.
  • Daily onboard presentations, and data shared with the Galápagos National Park Directorate.
