Entry 01 — the station Liveaboard expeditions · 8 and 15 days

Two seamounts at the top of the archipelago, reachable only by boat.

Darwin and Wolf sit alone in open Pacific water north of the main islands. There is no land to dive from and no day boat that reaches them. You arrive by liveaboard, after an overnight crossing, and you stay for three full days.

The audience for this page already knows what those two names mean. So this is written as the record it is: the transect we run, the sites we dive, the vessels we run it on, the animals actually logged there, and the research the observations feed. Sixteen guests. One shark scientist aboard every Citizen Science departure.

Whale sharks are at Darwin between June and November, and at the Arch almost every recorded animal is an adult female in apparent advanced pregnancy — which is the whole reason the Galápagos Whale Shark Project works there, and the reason a photograph taken by a guest can matter.

Entry 06 — method

What a guest actually contributes, and how it is checked.

Galapagos Shark Diving has supported the Galápagos Whale Shark Project since 2019 — satellite tags and tracking time, contributions to Sharkbook.ai, field logistics and data analysis. Photo-identification can involve guests. Satellite tagging and in-water behavioural research are scientist-led, and we keep those roles separate.

  1. 01A GWSP shark scientist is aboard every Citizen Science departure and briefs the water work before it happens.
  2. 02Observations are recorded to a standardised count protocol — SharkCount — rather than to memory.
  3. 03Photographs are taken of the spot pattern behind the gills, which is unique to the individual and identifies her again.
  4. 04Suitable images are submitted to Sharkbook.ai, the photo-identification database the project contributes to.
  5. 05Submissions are reviewed before they enter the research record. Only accepted contributions count.

† The spot pattern behind a whale shark's gills is unique to the individual and stable over her life. The matching technique descends from software written to match star fields; Sharkbook.ai is the database the project contributes to.
