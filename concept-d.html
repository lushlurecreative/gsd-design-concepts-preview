Galapagos Shark Diving operates liveaboard expeditions to the Darwin and Wolf seamounts, in the north of the Galápagos Marine Reserve. Eight-day and fifteen-day programmes, two vessels, sixteen guests to a departure. Whale shark season runs June to November. A Galápagos Whale Shark Project scientist has sailed on every Citizen Science departure since 2019.

03ResearchGalápagos Whale Shark Project · partnership since 2019

The whale shark
is listed
Endangered.

Nearly all of the whale sharks recorded at Darwin are large adult females. Since 2019 Galapagos Shark Diving has placed one Galápagos Whale Shark Project scientist on every Citizen Science departure, and funded satellite tags, tracking time and field logistics. Guests contribute photo identifications and standardised counts. The data goes to the Galápagos National Park Directorate and to Sharkbook.ai.

3.1

Photo-identification

Gill-region spot patterns matched against Sharkbook.ai, the international whale shark photo-ID database.

3.2

Satellite tagging

Tags and tracking time funded by Galapagos Shark Diving since 2019.

3.3

In-water behavioural research

Observation of adult female aggregations at Darwin during the June–November season.

3.4

SharkCount

Standardised observation and count protocols recorded by guests across archipelago dive sites.

3.5

Dive Against Debris

Underwater debris survey data contributed through Project AWARE.
