Expedition brief — Darwin & Wolf

This is not a beginner's ocean. Come equipped for it.

Eight days on a liveaboard at the northern seamounts of the Galápagos Marine Reserve: sixteen dives, currents of one to five knots, water at 16–24 °C, and a shark researcher in the water with you. Everything below is what the diving actually demands and exactly how you will be equipped for it.

01 Readiness Certification · logs · currency · paperwork

What the expedition asks of you before you board.

The requirement is not a formality. Darwin and Wolf are open-ocean seamounts with real current, real surge and real surface conditions, and the certification bar differs between our two expeditions. Read the one you intend to book.

Citizen Science ExpeditionAqua

A valid internationally recognised dive licence equivalent to Advanced Diver (PADI) — or an Open Water licence plus a Deep Diver specialty course — with more than 50 logged dives.

A valid licence equivalent to Advanced Diver (PADI) with more than 50 logged dives. The Open Water plus Deep Diver alternative is not accepted on this expedition.

  • ✓Certification card and logbookAt booking
  • ✓Completed dive forms3 months prior
  • ✓NitroxIncluded
Non-negotiable, both expeditions
  • Maximum depth 30 m / 100 ft. Decompression and solo diving are not allowed under any conditions.
  • At least one dive within the six months before your departure date. If you have been dry longer than that, do a refresher at home first.
  • A personal dive computer. Not optional, not shared, not rented as an afterthought.
  • Dive-accident insurance. DAN or Aquamed recommended. Travel insurance is separate and also your own.
  • Motion-sickness preparation. Bring medication or Transderm patches from your own doctor — the crossing to Darwin is open water.

If you are not there yet, say so when you enquire. We would rather move you to a later departure, or point you at a different Galápagos liveaboard through our advisory service, than put you on a Darwin dive you are not ready for.

02 Conditions June – November season

The numbers you are dressing for.

Galápagos water is colder than divers expect and the current is stronger than they expect. These are the figures we publish, not softened ones — plan your thermal protection against the bottom of each range, not the top.

Thermal protection, as we actually recommend it

Most divers find a 7 mm wetsuit from June through November, with a hooded vest suitable. Gloves and hood are strongly recommended. A 3 mm suit alone will not carry you through four dives a day at Wolf.

Note: the Galápagos National Park applies general restrictions on gloves. This is a live operational question we will answer honestly for your specific departure rather than paper over here.

→ Arriving

If you fly to Baltra on the sailing day, be on a flight that lands no later than 10:30 local time. Guides collect divers at the airport at 11:00. Staying in Puerto Ayora the night before: be back at Baltra by 11:00, or head straight to Baltra port.

← Leaving

On the day the trip ends, book a flight leaving no earlier than 12:00. Standard practice is to allow a further night before any international flight after a week of repetitive diving.

≡ Before sailing

Dive forms are due one month before a Citizen Science departure and three months before a Luxury Science departure. Flag dietary requirements in advance; some specific brands are not available on board.

06 The water What you are diving among, and what you are recording

Wildlife, season, and the work you do in the water.

A shark scientist from the Galápagos Whale Shark Project has been in the water on every Citizen Science departure since 2019. You are not watching research happen from the rail — your photographs go into the identification record.

  • Whale sharkAdult females, at the northern seamounts. IUCN Red List: Endangered.Jun – Nov
  • Scalloped hammerheadDaytime schooling aggregations at Wolf and Darwin.Season-long
  • Galápagos sharkResident, in numbers, across the northern circuit.Season-long
  • Oceanic manta rayCape Marshall and City of Mantas, on the walls.Season-long
  • Mola molaCold-water cleaning station at Punta Vicente Roca.Cold water
  • Marine iguanaFeeding underwater at Cabo Douglas, Fernandina — nowhere else on earth.Cabo Douglas
  • Green turtle · sea lion · Galápagos penguinThroughout the circuit, most reliably on the western sites.Throughout

Over 30 shark species have been identified in Galápagos waters, and nearly 99% of the whale sharks visiting these seamounts are adult females — which is precisely why the research happens here.

Your work in the water
  • Photo-identification. Gill-region spot patterns for whale sharks, facial patterns for turtles, ventral spots for mantas. Submitted to Sharkbook.ai.
  • Shark Count. Standardised observation protocol across archipelago dive sites, recorded dive by dive.
  • Dive Against Debris. Underwater debris data collection on the circuit.
  • Daily presentations from the researcher on board — satellite tagging, photo-ID and in-water behavioural work.
