Science · participation

The photograph you take can become part of the record.

Every whale shark carries a unique pattern of spots behind her gills. Photographed from the right angle it may identify that individual again. On Citizen Science departures, a GWSP shark scientist guides the process; submitted images are reviewed before they can become an accepted contribution.

What you may meet

Wild animals in open ocean. We record what we see and promise nothing, which is rather the point.

Whale shark
Wild animals in open ocean. Encounters are never guaranteed.
Scalloped hammerhead
Schools may be encountered in current at the northern islands. Sightings are never guaranteed.
Galápagos shark
May be recorded during SharkCount observations. Sightings are never guaranteed.
Giant manta
A possible encounter on the wider circuit, never a guaranteed sighting.
Mola alexandrini
A possible encounter on the wider circuit, never a guaranteed sighting.
Marine iguana
A possible encounter on the wider circuit, never a guaranteed sighting.
Silky shark
Typically seen in open water near seamounts, sometimes in large schools.
Whitetip reef shark
Often found resting in caves and under ledges by day, sometimes stacked with other individuals - a good first-shark-encounter species for nervous divers since it rarely moves when approached calmly.
Spotted eagle ray
Seen around most islands, feeding on hard-shelled benthic invertebrates.
Golden cownose ray
Highly social, often seen schooling; a bottom feeder that vacuum-sucks prey and crushes shells. Has a venomous tail barb used only defensively.
Bottlenose dolphin
Seen both at the surface and occasionally mid-dive. Highly acrobatic, with breaching, tail-slapping and bow-riding common, and can trigger visible bioluminescence at night.
Orca (killer whale)
Visitor sightings are explicitly infrequent. Documented prey in Galapagos waters includes sea lions, dolphins, fish, and, very rarely, green sea turtles.
Galapagos sea lion
Very comfortable around divers and snorkelers, often playful. Can dive up to about 10 minutes; predators include orcas and sharks. Standard diver guidance is to keep about 2m of distance.
Galapagos fur seal
Prefers shaded rocky coastlines and is notably shyer than the sea lion. Dives much longer than sea lions, especially at night, with dive duration tied to moon phase - longer on dark, new-moon nights.
Green sea turtle ("black turtle" locally)
Seen around every island, year-round, and considered among the calmer, more approachable species for divers.
Galapagos penguin
Genuinely swims with divers and snorkelers at sites like Cabo Douglas and Bartolome - fast and agile underwater.
Flightless cormorant (Galapagos cormorant)
An excellent diver that propels itself with its feet, then dries its wings on rocks after fishing despite being unable to fly.
Blue-footed booby
Hunts by plunge-diving from up to 80-100ft at around 60mph, sometimes diving 80ft or deeper underwater - skull air sacs cushion the impact.
Nazca booby
Courtship includes a distinctive sky-pointing display and gift-giving.
King angelfish
Functions as a cleaner fish for hammerhead sharks and manta rays at cleaning stations, directly connecting to those flagship species' own profiles. Forms monogamous, lifelong pairs.
Galapagos grouper ("Bacalao")
Seen on reef and rocky-bottom dives across the islands.
Sally lightfoot crab ("Zayapa")
Extremely agile, able to run in any direction and jump rock to rock, effectively appearing to walk on water in short hops. Its generalist diet (algae, carrion, picking ticks off marine iguanas, scavenging sea lion placenta) provides a real ecosystem cleanup service.
Pencil (slate pencil) sea urchin
A nocturnal grazer that emerges at dusk specifically to avoid daytime predators like hogfish, triggerfish and sea stars. Each spine cluster can host 20 or more other species as epifauna.
Conservation · accountability

Support that reaches the field.

Since 2019, GSD has supported the Galápagos Whale Shark Project through funding for tags and tracking time, contributions to Sharkbook.ai, field logistics and data analysis.

How the support works

Research without theatre.

Photo identification can involve guests. Satellite tagging and in-water behavioural research are scientist-led. We keep those roles separate and do not turn an uncertain result into a marketing claim.
