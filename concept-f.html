01BaltraEmbarkation · Santa Cruz

The course begins at an airstrip.

Dive guides meet guests at Baltra airport at 11:00 on the day of sailing. Flights in must land no later than 10:30; flights home on the final day leave no earlier than 12:00. Anyone staying in Puerto Ayora returns to Baltra by 11:00, or heads straight to the port by taxi. The first afternoon is a single check dive off Baltra northeast — weights sorted, camera housings flooded or not, before the long run north.

03Darwin01°40′N 92°00′W

The reason the course is drawn this way.

Darwin's Arch partially collapsed in 2021 and the remaining pillars are now called the Pillars of Evolution. The site is the anchor of every GSD itinerary: six dives here on the 8-day expedition, and between thirteen and eighteen on the 15-day. Whale sharks — overwhelmingly large adult females — pass through from June to November.

04Wolf01°23′N 91°49′W

The second seamount, and the current that comes with it.

Wolf holds some of the highest shark biomass anywhere in the archipelago, and strong current to go with it: scalloped hammerheads in schools, Galápagos sharks, silkies. Five dives here on the 8-day and nine on the 15-day. From Isabela the run up to Wolf takes about fourteen hours.

Chart note A · Work carried out on passage

Every Citizen Science departure carries a scientist.

Since 2019, Galapagos Shark Diving has placed a Galápagos Whale Shark Project scientist on every Citizen Science expedition. Guests are part of the method rather than an audience for it.

  • Photo-IDWhale sharks are identified from the spot pattern behind the gills; sea turtles from facial scale patterns, manta rays from ventral spots. Submissions go to Sharkbook.ai.
  • SharkCountStandardised observation and count protocols, logged dive by dive.
  • Satellite taggingTags fitted in the water, tracked afterwards; field logistics and analysis funded by the company.
  • Dive Against DebrisUnderwater debris surveys, submitted through Project AWARE.

05Punta Vicente RocaCabo Douglas · Fernandina

South again, into cold water.

One dive at Punta Vicente Roca on Isabela — a cold-water cleaning station for mola, also penguins, seahorses and the endemic Galápagos bullhead shark. One dive at Cabo Douglas on Fernandina, where marine iguanas graze underwater: the only lizard on earth that feeds at sea.

06Cousin's RockSanta Cruz Highlands

Two last dives, then dry land.

Two dives at Cousin's Rock off Santiago, then ashore to the Santa Cruz highlands: giant tortoises in the wild, a lava tunnel, and Los Gemelos. The 15-day expedition adds Roca Blanca, Pizón, Beagle Rocks, Marchena and Roca Redonda, plus one night dive. The course closes where it opened, at Baltra.
