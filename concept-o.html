Liveaboard expeditions

Darwin and Wolf

Eight days · Sixteen dives · A scientist aboard

The expedition

Eight days in the far north of the Galápagos, with a shark scientist aboard.

Darwin and Wolf lie in the far north of the archipelago. Thirty-four species of shark have been recorded in Galápagos waters, and between June and November the adult whale sharks arrive here — almost entirely mature females, nine to fourteen metres long, an aggregation documented nowhere else in the world.

Sixteen dives across the eight days, two to four a day, in water of sixteen to twenty-four degrees and currents of one to five knots. A Galápagos Whale Shark Project scientist has sailed on every one of these departures since 2019.

The two islands

Darwin's arch fell partly into the sea in 2021. What stands is now called the Pillars of Evolution.

Four dives at Darwin, then Wolf to the south — high shark biomass and strong water, where the hammerhead schools hang in the current in numbers that make it difficult to look at anything else. Both are uninhabited, both are reached only by liveaboard, and neither appears on an ordinary Galápagos cruise.

Research

In partnership with the Galápagos Whale Shark Project, since 2019.

The whale shark is listed Endangered on the IUCN Red List. The project works by photo-identification — every animal carries a unique pattern of spots behind the gills — by satellite tagging, and by in-water behavioural observation. Guests contribute the photographs and the standardised counts. The identifications go to Sharkbook.ai and the data to the Galápagos National Park Directorate.

One project scientist sails on every departure. Not as a guest lecturer — as the person doing the work.

What is asked of you

Darwin and Wolf are not a first liveaboard. The requirements are not negotiable, and they are published rather than discussed on arrival.

  • Advanced Open Water, or Open Water with a Deep Diver specialty
  • More than fifty logged dives
  • One dive within the previous six months
  • A dive computer, and dive-accident insurance
  • No decompression diving. No solo diving.
