Percentage of tracked individuals remaining inside the reserve. Source: Peñaherrera-Palma et al. (2016), as published by GSD. These are three published season bands, drawn across the months each band covers — not twelve separate monthly measurements.

Research output

The Galápagos Whale Shark Project has been supported since 2019, with one of its scientists aboard every Citizen Science departure. Methods: photo-identification into Sharkbook.ai, satellite tagging, blood sampling, and — added in 2025 — in-water ultrasound.

Named sharks are registered in Sharkbook.ai, the international photo-identification database. Whale sharks are identified by the spot pattern behind the gills; turtles by facial pattern; manta rays by ventral spots.

Reported constraints on the 2024 season

Two limits were named in the field report and are reproduced here rather than omitted: adverse weather, and the ultrasound equipment struggling to penetrate the thick skin of adult whale sharks. Only one pregnant female whale shark has ever been examined by scientists — caught in a Taiwanese fishery in 1995, carrying 304 embryos, some up to 64 cm long. That single specimen is why the ultrasound work at Darwin matters.
