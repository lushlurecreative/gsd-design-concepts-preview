02

Every whale shark carries a unique pattern of spots behind her gills.

Photographed from the right angle, that pattern can identify one individual again — years later, an ocean away. On Citizen Science departures a Galápagos Whale Shark Project scientist guides the process in the water. Suitable images are reviewed before they can become an accepted contribution, and contributed identifications go to Sharkbook.ai, the international whale shark photo-identification database.

Galapagos Shark Diving has supported the Galápagos Whale Shark Project since 2019 — funding for satellite tags and tracking time, contributions to Sharkbook.ai, field logistics and data analysis. Photo identification can involve guests. Satellite tagging and in-water behavioural research are scientist-led. Those roles stay separate.

Wolf Island — shark theatre

Shark theatre.

Wolf carries extraordinary shark biomass and currents to match it. Darwin’s Arch — the Pillars of Evolution since the arch fell in 2021 — is the other half of the story. The eight-day expedition dives Darwin’s Arch six times and Wolf five. The fifteen-day goes back for thirteen to eighteen at Darwin, nine at Wolf, and one night dive.

04

The vessels

Sixteen aboard.

  1. I Aqua Citizen Science Expeditions · nine guest cabins · 16 guests
  2. II Galaxy Diver II Luxury Science Expeditions · 16 guests · Nitrox included

Eight days and fifteen days. Two to four dives a day, all meals, transfers within the Galápagos, tanks and weights, bilingual dive masters — and a shark researcher aboard. The fifteen-day adds Roca Blanca, Pizón, Beagle Rocks, Marchena and Roca Redonda, and roughly fourteen hours of open water between Isabela and Wolf.
