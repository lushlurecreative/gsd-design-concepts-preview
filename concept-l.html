Whale shark

Rhincodon typus

Authority

Endangered
Station

The Galápagos archipelago is the only place worldwide where over 90% of sightings are of adult females. Galápagos individuals average 9–14 m. Global population has declined over 50% in the last 75 years.

IDX-34Index · shark species recorded in the archipelagoOn file

Thirty-four species are catalogued for the Galápagos. Eight are listed as the most commonly encountered: silky shark (Carcharhinus falciformis), Galapagos shark (C. galapagensis), blacktip shark (C. limbatus), tiger shark (Galeocerdo cuvier), Galapagos bullhead shark (Heterodontus quoyi), whale shark (Rhincodon typus), scalloped hammerhead (Sphyrna lewini) and whitetip reef shark (Triaenodon obesus). The remaining twenty-six are recorded as rare, occasional, migratory or pelagic. Some of the operator's pages say "over 30" instead of 34; the discrepancy is held, not resolved.
